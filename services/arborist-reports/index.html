Arborist reports in Canberra that do their job

An arborist report in Canberra isn’t paperwork for its own sake. It’s the document that gets your development application approved, your protected-tree work permitted, or your risk formally managed. Abloom Tree Care’s consultancy arm produces clear, defensible reports written by an AQF 5 arborist who also climbs and works on trees every week. That field experience shows in the reports: practical recommendations, realistic protection measures, and conclusions a government officer can act on.

Reports and assessments we provide

  • Tree Management Plans (TMP) for DA submission – development application support for builders, architects, developers and homeowners, including tree protection plans for construction near retained trees. The project arborist is engaged to see your development works from start to finish.
  • VALID tree risk assessments – structured, defensible risk ratings for individual trees or whole sites, ideal for schools, strata and commercial landholders.
  • Tree health and condition assessments – what’s wrong with the tree, whether it can be saved, and exactly what to do next.
  • Root assessments – investigating root damage to structures, paving and services, and root health under changed conditions.
  • Pruning and maintenance plans – scheduled programs that keep a tree population healthy and compliant over time.
  • Disease & infestation management – identification and treatment plans for the pests that affect Canberra’s trees.

Common Questions

Arborist consultancy questions, answered

Do I need an arborist report for my DA in Canberra?

If your development is near protected or significant trees, on your block, a neighbour’s, or government land, the approval authority will very likely require a tree assessment and protection plan as part of the application. We prepare DA-ready Tree Management Plans and can liaise with your architect or certifier directly.

What is a VALID tree risk assessment?

VALID is a structured, internationally recognised method for assessing the risk a tree poses, combining the likelihood of failure, the likelihood of impact and the consequences into a defensible risk rating. It gives landholders, schools and strata bodies a documented basis for deciding what to do about a tree.

What makes a tree protected in Canberra?

Under the Urban Forest Act 2023, a tree on leased land is regulated if it is at least 8m high, has a canopy at least 8m wide, has a trunk circumference greater than 1m measured 1.4m above natural ground level, or was planted in the last five years under a canopy contribution agreement or as a condition of a development approval. A dead native tree with a trunk circumference of at least 1.88m at that height is also regulated. Trees declared a pest species under the Biosecurity (Pests) Declaration 2025 are not regulated, even if they meet the size requirements.

How long does an arborist report take?

Straightforward single-tree reports are usually delivered within days of the site inspection. Larger jobs, such as multi-tree DA packages or whole-site risk assessments, take longer, and we’ll commit to a delivery date when we quote. Tell us your DA or court deadline and we’ll work to it.
